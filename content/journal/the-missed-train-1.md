After we met, everything seemed to fall into place. It all started around midnight. Those late-night conversations slowly turned into trust, and with every passing day, we shared more of ourselves. I found myself telling her almost everything, and no matter what I threw at her, she always seemed to have the perfect response. She understood me in a way that felt rare, and that made me trust her even more.

One thing I admired about her was that she never tried to interfere with my work. She gave me space while still making me feel heard. Every conversation felt effortless, and I genuinely looked forward to them.

But over time, we slowly began running out of conversations. That was probably the most heartbreaking part of the journey. As a software engineering student, my schedule became increasingly demanding. I had to balance our relationship with my career, and I knew I couldn’t pour all of my time into one thing, especially in the beginning. I started dedicating my nights to studying after 12:00 AM. We would usually spend around two hours talking, and once the conversation ended, I’d quietly return to my work. It wasn’t because I wanted to distance myself—it was because I had responsibilities I couldn’t ignore.

Things stayed that way until misunderstandings slowly began to appear. Most of them revolved around my friends. Looking back, the very first thing that attracted me to her wasn’t her appearance or her beauty—it was her understanding. I believed that was the strongest part of who she was. Her ability to understand people made me feel safe.

But at some point, that same understanding seemed to disappear when it came to me. She started feeling insecure about some of the friends I had on social media. From my perspective, there was never a reason for those insecurities. I tried my best to reassure her and understand her feelings on a deeper level instead of dismissing them. I wanted to be patient, supportive, and present.

Unfortunately, despite all my efforts, those insecurities kept growing, and little by little, they created distance between us. That’s when I felt our separation truly began—not because we stopped caring, but because the trust and understanding that had brought us together slowly started to fade.
