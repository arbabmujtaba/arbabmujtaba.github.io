After everything that happened, life took a rather unexpected turn. I always thought things would somehow work out, that I had enough time to figure everything out. But eventually, reality caught up with me. I began questioning my choices, wondering what I could have done differently and, more importantly, what I was supposed to do next.

I could have spent all my time trying to bring things back to how they used to be. Perhaps I should have tried harder; perhaps there were things I could have done differently. I still don't have all the answers. But somewhere along the way, I decided to put that energy into myself. Surprisingly, that was also when I landed my first internship. It's funny how life sometimes gives you something new just when you're struggling to make sense of what you've lost.

I spoke to people, searched for answers, and tried to understand what someone else might have done in my place. But none of their answers really felt right. Eventually, I realized that no amount of effort can make something work if only one person is willing to hold it together. Still, that doesn't mean I have no regrets. Sometimes I wonder if I could have done more, said more, or simply understood things a little earlier.

I did try to move forward, to meet new people and find new conversations. But some connections are difficult to explain, let alone replace. There was something about her that I couldn't quite find anywhere else. Maybe I realized that a little too late. Or maybe some things are simply meant to remain memories.

Meanwhile, I had a future to think about. Somewhere between college, sleepless nights, and the unfamiliar streets of Indore, I began building a version of myself I had never really imagined. I've sacrificed quite a lot for that future, and sometimes I wonder whether it'll all be worth it. But I suppose that's the price of chasing something you genuinely believe in. I'm still learning, still doubting myself, and still trying to become someone I can be proud of.

And yet, every now and then, I find myself at the port, watching the smoke rise. There's something strangely peaceful about it. I know I can't undo what happened, and perhaps that's the hardest part. I was so busy imagining the future that I never quite understood what I was leaving behind.

But the ship has sailed, or perhaps I was the one who set it on fire. Either way, there's little left to do but watch the smoke disappear and keep moving forward.

For now, I'll keep chasing the life I've always dreamed of and leave the rest to Allah. Some things are beyond our control, and perhaps learning to accept that is a part of growing up.
